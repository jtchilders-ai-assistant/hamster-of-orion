# Technology Tree Overview - Hamster of Orion

## Core Philosophy

The tech tree follows Master of Orion 1's elegant design:
- **Six technology fields** with independent progression
- **Tiered levels** within each field (roughly 8-10 levels per field)
- **Random selection** - not all techs are available each game
- **Miniaturization** - older techs become cheaper/smaller as you advance
- **Research choices** - typically 2-3 options per level per field

### 1. **Weapons**
Offensive systems for ships and ground combat
- Beam weapons (lasers, particle beams, plasma)
- Missile weapons (nuclear, fusion, antimatter)
- Bombs (for planetary bombardment)
- Special weapons (biological, death rays)

**Key for**: Combat races (Ferrets, Budgies, Guinea Pigs)

### 2. **Propulsion**
Engines, speed, and fuel efficiency
- Engine types (nuclear → fusion → antimatter → hyperspace)
- Speed bonuses
- Fuel range extensions
- Inertial stabilizers (maneuverability)

**Key for**: Expansionist races (Rabbits, Budgies, Chameleons)

### 3. **Construction**
Ship armor, factories, and building
- Armor types (titanium → duralloy → zortrium → neutronium)
- Factory construction bonuses
- Waste reduction
- Ship size increases

**Key for**: Production races (Ants, Mice, Guinea Pigs)

### 4. **Computers**
Targeting, ECM, scanning, and espionage
- Battle computers (accuracy bonuses)
- ECM (electronic countermeasures - defense)
- Planetary scanners (detect fleets)
- Spy tech (espionage bonuses)

**Key for**: Tactical races (Ferrets, Chameleons, Budgies)

### 5. **Force Fields**
Shields, planetary defenses, and barriers
- Ship shields (Class I → II → III → planetary)
- Planetary shields
- Repulsor beams (prevent bombardment)
- Stasis fields (disable weapons)

**Key for**: Defensive races (Hermit Crabs, Hamsters, Mice)

### 6. **Planetology**
Terraforming, environmental colonization, and population management
- Terraforming (increase max population)
- Soil Enrichment (increase planet size)
- Environmental adaptation (colonize hostile worlds)
- Ecological tech (pollution cleanup)
- Biological weapons

**Key for**: Growth races (Rabbits, Ants, Rats)

## Terminology Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Tech Level** | Numbered position (1-50) indicating research progression within a field |
| **Tier** | Research cost bracket (1-18) that determines base RP cost |
| **Research Cost** | Actual Research Points (RP) required to complete a technology (50 to 100,000+) |
| **Field** | One of the six technology categories (Weapons, Propulsion, Construction, Computers, Force Fields, Planetology) |
| **Miniaturization** | Cost/space reduction for components as your tech level exceeds component's tech level |

**Example:** Fusion Beam is Tech Level 20 in Weapons field, Tier 6, Research Cost ~2,000 RP.

## Tech Level Structure

Each field has roughly **8-10 tiers** of technology:

**Tier 1-2**: Early game (starting technologies, basics)
**Tier 3-4**: Early-mid game (establishing advantage)
**Tier 5-6**: Mid game (power spike)
**Tier 7-8**: Late game (devastating capabilities)
**Tier 9-10**: End game (victory-enabling, rare)

## Research Mechanics

### Research Points (RP)
- Generated by population working as "scientists"
- Modified by race bonuses (Rats +50%, Guinea Pigs -20%)
- Buildings add research (Research Labs)
- Some artifacts/events grant bonus RP

### Tech Costs
Early techs: 100-500 RP
Mid techs: 1,000-5,000 RP
Late techs: 10,000-50,000 RP
Ultimate techs: 100,000+ RP

Costs scale with game speed and difficulty.

### Selection Process
When you complete a tech tier, you're offered **2-3 random choices** for the next tier:
- Creates variability between games
- Forces strategic adaptation
- Some games you get lucky, some you don't
- Trading and espionage can fill gaps

### Miniaturization
As you advance in a field, older technologies:
- Take less space on ships (50% per tier)
- Cost less to produce
- Become baseline for new designs

Example:
- You research "Gatling Laser" (Tier 3 weapon)
- Your old "Laser Cannon" (Tier 2) now takes half the space
- Your old "Hand Laser" (Tier 1) is basically free

## Race-Specific Bonuses

Rather than unique tech trees, races get **bonuses when researching certain techs**:

### Example: Ferrets Research "Omega-V Bomb"
- Base cost: 10,000 RP
- Standard effect: Bomb dealing 500 damage
- **Ferret bonus**: Deals 625 damage (+25% from Deadly Accuracy)
- **Ferret version renamed**: "Precision Strike Omega-V"

### Example: Rats Research "Fusion Drive"
- Base cost: 3,000 RP
- **Rats pay**: 1,500 RP (50% research bonus)
- Rats finish tech faster, use savings to research more

### Example: Chameleons Research "Espionage School"
- Base effect: +10% spy success
- **Chameleon bonus**: +20% spy success (their natural +60% stacks)
- Chameleons get more value from spy techs

## Technology Trading

- Can trade technologies through diplomacy
- Some races refuse to trade military tech (Guinea Pigs, Ferrets)
- Some races eagerly trade (Hamsters, Rats, Mice)
- Trading establishes "tech exchange" relationships

## Technology Theft (Espionage)

- Chameleons excel at this
- Steal random tech from target empire
- More expensive techs are harder to steal
- Can steal techs you don't have available in your tree

## Orion System (Strategic Milestone)

Defeating the Guardian of Orion requires advanced technology:
- Advanced propulsion (reach Orion)
- Powerful weapons (defeat Guardian)
- Strong shields (survive Guardian)
- High-tier computers (target Guardian)

**Note**: Defeating the Guardian is NOT a victory condition, but grants major technological advantages that help achieve Diplomatic or Domination victory.

## Balancing Notes

### Fast Research Races (Rats, Mice)
- Get more techs, reach end-game first
- Compensates for military/production weaknesses
- Must survive early game to leverage

### Slow Research Races (Guinea Pigs, Rabbits)
- Get fewer techs, lag technologically
- Must win through other means (combat, numbers)
- Can steal/trade for critical techs

### Balanced Races (Hamsters, Hermit Crabs)
- Normal research pace
- Get average number of techs
- No major advantages or handicaps

## Tech Tree Philosophy

**MOO1-Style Elegance**:
- Six fields, independent progression
- Clean tiers, clear upgrades
- Random selection creates replayability
- Trading/spying fills gaps

**NOT like MOO2**:
- No tech prerequisites across fields
- No "creative/uncreative" race traits
- Simpler, more streamlined
- Focus on strategic choices, not complexity

**Pet-Themed Flavor**:
- Technology names reference the pet lore
- Ancient Ones' repurposed infrastructure
- "Habitrail Hyperspace Network"
- "Exercise Wheel Power Core"
- "Pellet Dispenser Mass Driver"

## Quick Reference: What Each Race Prioritizes

- **Budgies**: Propulsion, Computers (speed + targeting)
- **Guinea Pigs**: Weapons, Construction (bigger weapons, bigger ships)
- **Chameleons**: Computers (espionage), Force Fields (defense)
- **Hamsters**: Balanced (no priority)
- **Ants**: Construction, Planetology (production + growth)
- **Mice**: Construction, Computers (automation + tech)
- **Ferrets**: Weapons, Computers (damage + accuracy)
- **Rats**: ALL (research everything)
- **Rabbits**: Planetology, Propulsion (growth + expansion)
- **Hermit Crabs**: Force Fields, Construction (defense + armor)
