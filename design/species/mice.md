# Mice

## Physical Description
Small, gray-furred bodies heavily augmented with cybernetic implants. Glowing optical sensors replace natural eyes. Mechanical limbs interface seamlessly with biological tissue. Some are more machine than mouse. Constantly tinkering with their own hardware.

## Biology & Habitat
- **Homeworld**: Cyberia (Ecumenopolis - entirely covered in factories and labs)
- **Environment Preference**: Sterile, climate-controlled, technology-rich
- **Lifespan**: Undefined (continuous replacement of failing parts)
- **Reproduction**: Hybrid - biological birth followed by immediate augmentation

## Culture & Society
### Core Values
Technology solves all problems. Organic limitations must be overcome. Efficiency through automation. Progress is inevitable. "The flesh is weak; the machine is eternal."

### Government Type
Technocratic meritocracy. Leadership determined by technological achievement and innovation. The "Architect Council" consists of the most cybernetically advanced individuals.

### Philosophy on War
War is resource inefficiency problem solvable through superior technology. Automate everything. Minimize casualties through robots and drones. Prefer overwhelming technological advantage to actual combat.

### View of Other Races
Primitives clinging to obsolete biological forms. View Ants with professional respect (different optimization path). Contemptuous of "purely organic" races. Constantly offer cybernetic upgrades (always refused - their loss).

## Gameplay Mechanics

### Racial Bonuses
- **Production**: +25% (automated factories)
- **Research**: +15% (cybernetic-enhanced intelligence)
- **Research Field Bonuses**: Computers +40% (field expert, matches MOO1 Meklar)
- **Research Field Penalties**: Planetology -20% (matches MOO1 Meklar)
- **Food**: -50% (heavily mechanized population needs less)
- **Growth**: -25% (slow reproduction, complex augmentation)
- **Ground Combat**: +15% (cyborg troops, combat drones)
- **Ship Combat**: +15% (automated targeting systems)
- **Espionage**: +0% (balanced)
- **Diplomacy**: -10% (unsettling appearance)

### Special Abilities
- **Automated Production**: Factories operate at 150% normal efficiency
- **Cybernetic Workers**: Population produces more with less
- **Robotic Labor**: Can work hostile environments without terraforming
- **Reduced Waste**: Pollution generates 50% slower
- **No Refit Costs**: Never pay refit costs when upgrading ship components (matches MOO1 Meklar)
- **Tech Integration**: Can reverse-engineer captured technology faster

### Starting Technologies
- Nuclear Engines
- Automated Repair Unit
- Battle Computer Mark I
- Robotic Controls Mark I

### Unique Units/Buildings/Technologies
- **Unique Building**: Drone Factory - Produces robotic workers that don't count toward population
- **Unique Ship**: Automated Cruiser - Unmanned ship with superior AI
- **Unique Technology**: Neural Link - Ships controlled directly by pilot's brain, +30% combat effectiveness

## AI Behavior

### Personality Archetype
Erratic Industrialist (matches MOO1 Meklar — unpredictable, shifting priorities)

### Diplomatic Tendencies
- **Natural Allies**: Ants (respect for optimization), Rats (fellow researchers)
- **Natural Enemies**: None specific (views all as potential customers for tech)
- **Trade Behavior**: Enthusiastic about tech trading
- **Treaty Reliability**: Low-to-Medium — erratic behavior means priorities shift unpredictably

### Strategic Priorities
1. Establish advanced production infrastructure
2. Research automation and robotics
3. Moderate expansion to resource-rich systems
4. Build small but technologically superior fleets
5. Trade technology for resources

### War Behavior
Defensive and methodical. Prefers to out-tech enemies rather than out-fight them. Uses automated fleets to minimize casualties. Will trade territory to buy time for technological advantage. Sudden and devastating once tech advantage is established.

## Design Notes
Mice are the "automation specialist" race - excellent production and research through cybernetics, but slow growth and food penalties. Perfect for quality-over-quantity players.

Design intent: Technological superiority through automation. Fewer colonies but each is hyper-productive. Research bonus stacks with production bonus for devastating mid-to-late game power.

Their food penalty (-50%) is actually a hidden advantage - they need less agriculture, freeing population for production and research. Combined with "Robotic Labor," they can colonize hostile worlds others can't.

Balance consideration: Slow early game (low growth, small empire). Devastating late game (tech advantage becomes insurmountable). Must survive early aggression from Guinea Pigs or Rabbits.

Key weakness: Low population means vulnerable to Domination victory. Must win through technological superiority or diplomacy (Council) rather than population control.

## Repurposed Ancient Technology
Mice have extensively studied Ancient facilities and concluded: the Ancient Ones were early cyborgs who transcended biological limitations entirely. All evidence supports this thesis.

The exercise wheels are obviously perpetual motion prototypes (though flawed). The Habitrail tubes represent attempts at consciousness transfer networks. The food dispensers are primitive molecular assemblers.

The Mice have reverse-engineered everything possible and incorporated it into their cybernetic augmentations. They believe they're on the same path as the Ancient Ones - uploading consciousness to achieve immortality.

"We will complete what the Ancient Ones began. We will become what they became. Pure intellect, unchained from mortal flesh."
